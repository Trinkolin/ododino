In a rock face of Mount Mladesiena, above the village of Antro in the municipality of Pulfero, opens a cave with a church inside it. This is San Giovanni d'Antro: Landarska jama in Slovene, Svet Ivan u čelè in the Slovene dialect of the valleys, San Zuan di Landri in Friulian. It is one of the most remarkable places in the Natisone Valleys.

## The cave

The entrance lies at 348 metres above sea level and is reached by the 114 steps of a stone staircase built against the cliff. Inside, a stream runs through a succession of small lakes, chambers, meanders and shafts. The cave has been explored for about 4 km, but only the first 300 metres are equipped for visitors.

Remains of cave bears, pre-Roman and Roman pottery and medieval metal objects have been found here. In Roman times the cave was part of the system defending the eastern frontier; during the barbarian invasions it sheltered the local population. Its first permanent inhabitant was probably a Christian hermit, around the 5th or 6th century.

## The church in the rock

The first document mentioning a place of worship dates from 889, when King Berengar granted the deacon Felix the church of Antro and the surrounding land. The cave still holds the deacon's 9th-century Latin tombstone.

In the 12th–13th centuries the cave was fortified as an outpost of a castle below. Once it became purely a place of worship, the outer staircase and a chapel dedicated to Saints John the Baptist and John the Evangelist were added in the 15th century. An inscription dates the chapel to **1477**: it was built by master Andrej of Škofja Loka, with a star vault in the late Slovene Gothic style. The frescoes, from 1530, are attributed to Jernej of Škofja Loka or his workshop.

At the end of the 17th century the cave's great hall, about 16 by 10 metres with walls and vault of bare rock, became the main church. At its far end stands a Baroque gilded wooden altar (*zlati oltar*), attributed to master Jernej Vrtav of the Kobarid school.

## The legend of Queen Vida

The valleys tell how Queen Vida, who lived in the castle of Biacis, took refuge in the cave with her people to escape Attila. After months of siege, with a single sack of grain left, she threw it from the cliff, shouting that she had as many sacks left as there were grains. Attila believed the besieged had endless supplies and gave up. The historian Pier Silverio Leicht concluded that the legend derived from stories told elsewhere in northern Italy; either way, it lives on in Slovene nursery rhymes and poetry.

## Good to know

The cave is visited with a guide; opening times and bookings on the official website [grottadantro.it](https://www.grottadantro.it/). CAI trail 752 to Montefosca also starts from Antro.
