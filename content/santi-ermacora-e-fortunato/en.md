Savogna, the village from which the road up the Matajur climbs, is gathered around its parish church, dedicated to Saints Hermagoras and Fortunatus (Santi Ermacora e Fortunato in Italian, Sv. Mohor in Fortunat in Slovene).

## A church born of war

During the First World War the old church of Savogna was used as a storehouse. For that reason it was set on fire in 1917, during the retreat that followed the defeat at Caporetto. The present church was built in 1919 and consecrated in 1921 by Antonio Anastasio Rossi, Archbishop of Udine. It became a parish in 1955 and was renovated in 2002.

In the liturgy, **Slovene** is used alongside Italian.

## What to see

The east-facing façade is reached by a short flight of steps: four pilasters divide it into three parts and support a triangular pediment pierced by an oculus. Inside, a single nave of five bays has a barrel vault; the chancel, raised by three steps, has a cross vault painted blue, beige and pink. The most valuable work is an 18th-century canvas of the Virgin and Child with a kneeling saint.

## Nearby

CAI trail **755** starts at the church and climbs about 3 km to Monte San Canziano (723 m). The municipality has other churches, including Santa Lucia "in monte" at Brizza, at 581 metres, built in the first half of the 16th century.

## Good to know

On 12 July, the feast of Saints Hermagoras and Fortunatus, Savogna holds its village festival with music and local theatre. Dates may change: check locally.
