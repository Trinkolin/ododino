The **Alpe Adria Trail** is a long-distance walk of about 750 km in 43 stages, from the foot of the Großglockner, Austria's highest mountain, to the Adriatic at Muggia. It crosses three countries, Austria, Slovenia and Italy, and passes through the Natisone Valleys before descending to Cividale.

## A cultural trail

The route was developed by the tourism boards of Carinthia, Slovenia and Friuli Venezia Giulia and has been waymarked end to end since 2012 with its own logo. The alpine clubs of the three countries look after waymarking and maintenance: the ÖAV in Austria, the PZS in Slovenia and the CAI in Italy. It was conceived less as a sporting challenge than as a cultural route, linking existing, often historic paths from village to village. Each stage is about 20 km and takes about 6 hours; at the end of most of them you will find places to eat and sleep.

## The stages in the valleys

After the Triglav National Park and the Soča valley, the trail leaves Slovenia near Tolmin along old military roads. According to the route mapped on OpenStreetMap, it has two stages in the valleys:

- **E27, Tolmin to Tribil Superiore**: about 18 km;
- **E28, Tribil Superiore to Cividale del Friuli**: about 24 km.

On the way it partly follows existing CAI trails, such as no. 747, the Natisone Valleys high route; the signposts carry the Alpe Adria Trail logo next to the CAI's red-and-white markings.

From Cividale the trail turns south, through the Collio hills and the Karst above Trieste, with a detour into Slovenia at Lipica, to Muggia.

## Good to know

Sources do not all agree on the route through the valleys: before setting out, download the up-to-date GPX track from the official website [alpe-adria-trail.com](https://www.alpe-adria-trail.com/).
