San Leonardo (Svet Lienart or Podutana in the local Slovene dialect, Sveti Lenart in Slovene) is the municipality of the Cosizza and Erbezzo valleys, two streams that meet near Scrutto before flowing, with the Alberone, into the Natisone. The village is first mentioned in 1257 and is named after its patron, Saint Leonard of Noblac, whose feast is on 6 November. It is a "scattered" municipality: the town hall is not here but in Merso di Sopra.

## The mother church

The church of San Leonardo (Sv. Lenart) was one of the most important religious centres of the valleys: founded perhaps before the year 1000 and subject to the chapter of Cividale, it certainly existed in the 12th century, though its first written mention dates from 1351. It was rebuilt in 1717, altered in 1777, when the bell tower was also reworked, then enlarged and consecrated in 1827. Damaged by the 1976 earthquake, it was restored between 1984 and 1986.

In the liturgy, Slovene and the local dialect, *po nasen*, are used alongside Italian.

## What to see

Seven steps lead up to the **porch** with its stone columns; on either side of the doorway, two niches hold busts. Inside, three aisles are separated by round arches on massive stone columns. The marble high altar has statues of Saints Joseph and Joachim and an 1827 altarpiece of Saints Leonard, Sebastian and Roch. The two **gilded wooden side altars** (*zlati oltar*), with paintings of the Crucifixion and Saint Apollonia, were made around 1710 in a workshop in Kobarid (Caporetto).

## Nearby

- CAI trail **766** starts at the town hall and leads in about 2 km to the **Kot waterfalls** (Melinski Kot), near the confluence of the streams: the second, the most admired, drops more than 12 metres.
- Near the waterfalls is the **Star Čedad** cave ("Old Cividale"), explored for 1,200 metres. Legend says Cividale once stood here, until a flood from the spring pushed it to where it is today.
- In Merso di Sopra, under the lime trees of the church of Sant'Antonio Abate, the assembly of the Merso "banca" met, one of the two self-governing bodies of the valleys; the lime tree and the stone slab still appear on the municipal coat of arms.
