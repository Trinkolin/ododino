Cividale del Friuli (Čedad in Slovene, Cividât in Friulian) is the gateway to the Natisone Valleys: it stands on the river, on the road that climbs from the Friulian plain towards the Soča (Isonzo). Founded by the Romans as *Forum Iulii*, the name from which the whole of Friuli takes its own, it became in 568 the capital of the first Lombard duchy in Italy.

## The Devil's Bridge

The city's symbol is the **Ponte del Diavolo**, the Devil's Bridge (Hudičev most in Slovene, *Velik muost*, "the big bridge", in the valleys' dialect), which spans the Natisone above a spectacular gorge. Built of stone, 48 metres long and 22.5 metres high, it has two arches of different widths: the central pier rests on a natural boulder in the middle of the river.

Work began in 1442, to a design by Iacopo Dugaro da Bissone, replacing a wooden crossing often swept away by floods, and dragged on for decades. On 27 October 1917, during the retreat from Caporetto, the Italian army blew it up; the Austrians rebuilt it in the same form and opened it on 18 May 1918.

The name comes from a legend: to build the bridge, the townspeople supposedly asked the Devil for help, and he demanded in return the soul of the first to cross it. The Devil built it in a single night, but in the morning the people sent an animal across first, a cat, a dog or a pig depending on the version. Others say the Devil merely placed the huge boulder on which the central pier stands.

## The Lombard Temple

A short walk away, in the monastery of Santa Maria in Valle, the **Tempietto longobardo** (oratory of Santa Maria in Valle) is the most important and best preserved architectural monument of the Lombard period. Built around the middle of the 8th century as the chapel of the gastald's palace, it is part of the site "Longobards in Italy: places of power", inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in June 2011; Cividale is the lead city of the site.

## Towards the valleys

From Cividale, CAI trail 775 climbs to the sanctuary of Castelmonte and trail 776 to San Pietro al Natisone; the last stage of the Alpe Adria Trail through the valleys, from Tribil Superiore, also ends here. In the pastry shops, look for **gubana**, the spiral cake of the Natisone Valleys, filled with walnuts, raisins and pine nuts.
