Between Cividale del Friuli and the mountains above Kobarid (Caporetto), the **Natisone Valleys** (Nediške doline in Slovene) are the heart of the historical region called **Benečija** in Slovene and **Slavia friulana** or **Slavia veneta** in Italian. Four valleys, the Natisone, Alberone, Cosizza and Erbezzo, and seven municipalities: Pulfero, San Pietro al Natisone, Savogna, San Leonardo, Stregna, Grimacco and Drenchia. Its emblematic mountain is the [Matajur](../matajur/).

## The names

Under the Republic of Venice the area was officially called *Schiavonia*, hence the Friulian *Sclavanie*. *Slavia veneta* and the Slovene *Beneška Slovenija*, "Venetian Slovenia", recall that very period; in Slovene today it is also simply called *Benečija*.

## A long history

The valleys have been inhabited since the late Palaeolithic. In Roman times the road to Noricum ran up the Natisone, and on it grew *Forum Iulii*, today's [Cividale](../cividale/), later the capital of the first Lombard duchy. Slavs settled in the valleys in a slow, steady influx from the late Carolingian period to the 14th century, living alongside the Romance-speaking population.

In the Middle Ages the valleys formed the gastaldate of Antro under the Patriarchs of Aquileia. In 1420 they passed to the Republic of Venice, which granted these poor and remote communities broad tax privileges and administrative and judicial self-government; their military duty was limited to guarding five border passes with two hundred men. Two people's courts, the **banche** of Antro and Merso, each of twelve judges, met around stone slabs, like the [stone of the Banca di Antro](../lastra-banca-di-antro/); the general assembly of the valleys, the **arengo**, was held by the church of [San Quirino](../san-quirino/).

Self-government ended with the fall of Venice in 1797 and the Napoleonic period, which abolished the banche and arenghi and created the municipalities. After a return to Austria, the valleys passed to the Kingdom of Italy in 1866. In 1917 the Battle of Caporetto swept over them: trenches and caves remain on [Monte Cum](../monte-cum/) and on the [Kolovrat](../rifugio-solarie/).

## The language

A Slovene dialect is spoken in the valleys, varying from one valley to another; Slavists classify it among the Slovene dialects of the Littoral group. The area's Slovene community is protected by Law no. 38 of 2001, although during its passage through Parliament some local forces opposed extending it to the valleys. The [SMO](../smo/) museum in San Pietro tells the story of this language and cultural landscape. In several churches Mass is also said in Slovene or in dialect.

## Places to discover

- Churches and historic sites: [San Giovanni d'Antro](../san-giovanni-d-antro/), the church in the cave; [San Quirino](../san-quirino/); the mother church of [San Leonardo](../san-leonardo/); [Santa Maria in Liessa](../santa-maria-liessa/); [Savogna](../santi-ermacora-e-fortunato/); [Monte San Martino](../monte-san-martino/).
- Mountains and trails: the [Matajur](../matajur/), [Monte Cum](../monte-cum/), the [Solarie hut and Zanuso bivouac](../rifugio-solarie/), the [Alpe Adria Trail](../alpe-adria-trail/).
