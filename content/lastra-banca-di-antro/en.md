In the small village of Biacis (Bijača in Slovene), in the municipality of Pulfero, a large stone slab is on display: the one around which the **Banca di Antro** sat, one of the two people's courts that administered justice in the Natisone Valleys for almost three centuries. Nearby, above the village of Antro, is the cave of [San Giovanni d'Antro](../san-giovanni-d-antro/).

## The valleys' "banche"

Under the Republic of Venice the valleys enjoyed broad self-government. At the beginning of the 16th century the territory was divided into two districts, Antro and Merso, each with its own court, the **banca**, made up of twelve lay judges. As in other parts of northern Italy and the German lands, the judges met around stone slabs or tables.

The Banca di Antro sat under the lime trees near Biacis, and at Tarcetta. It heard civil and criminal cases from the valleys at first instance, with the gastald present as guarantor; appeals went to the community of Cividale and then to the Venetian lieutenant. For murders, the elders of all the valleys met instead by the church of [San Quirino](../san-quirino/). The banche and assemblies were abolished in the Napoleonic period.

## The stone

At Antro the judges used an ancient slab, probably prehistoric, whose original purpose is still unknown. It is now on display in Biacis, under a shelter, next to a panel telling its story. A lime tree and a stone slab still appear on the coat of arms of the municipality of [San Leonardo](../san-leonardo/), in memory of the other banca, Merso's.

## Good to know

In Biacis, the church of San Giacomo dates in its present form from 1520, after the earthquakes of 1511 and 1513. In July the village celebrates Saint Anne and Saint James, and on the evening of 6 January the *kries* is lit, the bonfire read for omens for the new year. A nature trail links Biacis with Antro.
