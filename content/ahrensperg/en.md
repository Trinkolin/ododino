The small village of Biacis (Bijača in Slovene), in the municipality of Pulfero, keeps two reminders of the centuries when the Natisone Valleys were a frontier land with institutions of their own: the remains of **Ahrensperg castle** and the **stone slab of the Banca di Antro**. Nearby, above the village of Antro, is the cave of [San Giovanni d'Antro](../san-giovanni-d-antro/).

## Ahrensperg castle

The castle controlled the road climbing from Cividale towards Carinthia; it was probably built on the foundations of a Lombard fortification. It is first mentioned in 1149 and, in 1251, as a castle of the Patriarchs of Aquileia, who entrusted it to vassals. In 1306 it was besieged by Count Henry III of Gorizia, and in the 14th century it changed hands several times between the patriarchs and the counts of Gorizia.

In 1364, during the wars between the patriarch and the Duke of Austria, it was destroyed, like the nearby castles of Zuccola, Urusbergo and Antro; on 3 April 1365 the Parliament of the Patria del Friuli decided that the destroyed castles should not be rebuilt. The walls were used as building material; only the **square tower**, still visible today, survived. In 1567 a visitor described it as being used by the people of the valleys as a prison. The historic value of the ruins was recognised in 1909 and the tower was first restored in 1927.

## The stone of the Banca di Antro

Under Venice the valleys had two people's courts, the **banche** of Antro and Merso, set up at the beginning of the 16th century, each of twelve judges. As in other parts of northern Italy and the German lands, the judges met around stone slabs or tables. At Antro an ancient slab was used, probably prehistoric, whose original purpose is still unknown. The Banca di Antro sat here, under the lime trees near Biacis, and at Tarcetta: it heard civil and criminal cases from the valleys at first instance, with the gastald present as guarantor.

The slab is now on display in Biacis, under a shelter, next to a panel telling its story.

## Good to know

In Biacis, the church of San Giacomo dates in its present form from 1520, after the earthquakes of 1511 and 1513. In July the village celebrates Saint Anne and Saint James, and on the evening of 6 January the *kries* is lit, the bonfire read for omens for the new year. A nature trail links Biacis with Antro.
