title: Monte San Martino

---
Monte San Martino (Svet Martin in Slovene) rises to 987 metres in the municipality of Grimacco, between the Cosizza and Alberone valleys. It is a modest mountain, but its summit overlooks two valleys: for centuries it has been both a place of worship and a lookout.

## Getting to the top

The shortest way starts from the **Priavelo pass** (663 m): a path or a mule track climbs through chestnut woods and meadows full of daisies, lilies of the valley and crocuses. The top consists of two grassy terraces: the chapel stands on the first, at 965 metres; the summit is on the second, a little further on. Near the pass, at 679 metres, the **Baita CAI Val Natisone**, opened in 2016, is a two-storey stone shelter.

For a longer hike:

- CAI trail **761** climbs from Scrutto in the Alberone valley, via Clastra, the San Martino pass and the summit, to the Topolò saddle (bocchetta di Topolò); about 5 hours. At the saddle it meets trail 745, which descends to Polava or the village of Topolò;
- route no. 7 of the **Sentieri di pace** ("paths of peace") is a 13 km loop from Clodig via the Priavelo, the summit and Topolò, with about 734 metres of ascent. Easy on foot, demanding by mountain bike.

## The chapel

The chapel of San Martino is Romanesque and dates from the 13th century. It is first mentioned in a feudal deed of 1299, which refers to the church's feast "in monte S. Martini". The site was frequented long before: in 1998 a hoard of nine Late Antique tools was found here.

## 1848

Lying between two valleys and close to a pass, the mountain has always been a watch post. On 21 and 22 April 1848, during the First Italian War of Independence, local volunteers occupied it to stop the Austrian troops who had come down from the Luico pass and were marching on the insurgent city of Udine. When Udine surrendered, their resistance became pointless and the group disbanded. A memorial stone near the chapel recalls the event.

## The festival

Every year, on the third Sunday of September, a traditional festival of the valleys' Slovene community (*kuatarinca*) takes place around the chapel, drawing worshippers from far afield. Dates may change: check locally.
