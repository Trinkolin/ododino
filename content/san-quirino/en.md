Just outside San Pietro al Natisone stands the small church of San Quirino (Sv. Kvirin in Slovene). It is the oldest in the municipality and one of the most history-laden places in the valleys: for centuries, the assembly of the Slovene communities of the Natisone met here.

## A site inhabited since ancient times

The church stands on an Iron Age necropolis, with cremation graves and grave goods, later reused in Roman and Lombard times. Tradition has it that it was built on the ruins of a temple of Diana, but this is only a hypothesis.

A church dedicated to Saint Quirinus of Siscia is recorded as early as 1250. At the end of the 15th century it was rebuilt in the late Slovene Gothic style of the Škofja Loka school: a stone with a Gothic inscription on the outer wall names the builder, master Martin Petrich (Pirich in Slovene), and gives the date 1494, while other sources say 1493.

## The assembly under the lime trees

For centuries the valleys enjoyed broad self-government, organised into two "banche", Antro and Merso. Under the lime trees next to the church met the **Great Arengo**, the assembly that elected representatives and dealt with the common affairs of the two valleys. Its last meeting was held on 2 May 1804, shortly before Napoleon's arrival put an end to local self-government.

In the 19th century the church was nearly demolished to enlarge the one at Azzida; the plan was dropped within about ten years. Damaged by the 1976 earthquake, it is well preserved today.

## What to see

A porch stands before the entrance, and a bell gable with two bells crowns the roof. The doorway is pointed. Inside, the nave has an open timber roof; a pointed arch leads into the polygonal chancel, covered by a net-ribbed vault. At the rib crossings you can make out the sun, the Virgin and Child, Saint John the Evangelist, Saint Quirinus in bishop's robes and two coats of arms, of Cividale or of the Gastaldia of Antro. The altarpiece of Saints Quirinus, Sebastian and Roch is by the Cividale painter Luigi Bront, from the early 20th century.

Today the church is a votive shrine to the fallen of both World Wars.

## Good to know

The **Trail nelle Valli del Natisone**, a loop of about 26 km, passes close by, and CAI trail 776 links San Pietro with Cividale. The sources give no opening times for the church: ask locally.
